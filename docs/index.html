The method — how the ledger thinks

Payments apply oldest-first. When money comes in, it kills the oldest open charge first — that stops per-diem fees from accruing on the longest-running debt. It's the landlord-friendly convention, and it's how courts tend to apply partial rent.

Fees trigger the day after grace ends. Paid on or before due date + grace = zero fees, no exceptions. One day past grace = the full flat fee, plus per-diem for every day past grace until that charge is fully paid, plus the monthly percentage on the balance still outstanding every 30 days.

Unpaid charges accrue. A charge nobody has paid keeps growing its per-diem and monthly percentage fees up to today's date — that's the "still bleeding" red row. The fee cap, if you set one, is the ceiling per charge.

Days late measures settlement, not arrival. A partial payment doesn't stop the clock — days late counts from the end of grace until the charge is fully settled. That's why paying in dribs and drabs is expensive: every day of delay is per-diem.
